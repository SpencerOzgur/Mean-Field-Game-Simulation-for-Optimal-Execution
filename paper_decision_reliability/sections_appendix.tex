\section{Reference worlds}
\label{app:worlds}

Both mechanisms run on the same price--time-priority matching engine as the Hawkes simulator, with a 10\,bps tick and lots of 100 shares, and are simulated by thinning state-independent candidate streams.

\paragraph{R1, queue-reactive.} Order flow has no memory of past events; every intensity is a function of the current queues \citep{huang2015queue}. Joining the best quote becomes less likely as that queue grows; limit orders arrive behind the best at a constant rate; quotes are improved only when the spread is at least two ticks; cancellations are proportional to resting depth; and a market order is more likely when the queue it would hit is short relative to the opposite best queue. A large sell thins the bid, which attracts both more selling and new bids. R1a is deep with a weak imbalance effect; R1b is thinner with a strong one.

\paragraph{R2, behavioural agent mix with a hidden regime.} Noise traders submit limit orders, market orders and cancellations at constant rates. Fundamentalists send market orders towards a latent fundamental value that follows a random walk, with intensity growing as the 1.5 power of the mispricing. Trend followers send market orders in the direction of the recent return and are several times more active in a hidden ``trending'' regime that switches as a two-state Markov chain. Liquidity providers cancel quotes on a side that has just absorbed one-sided market volume. The fundamental and the regime are exogenous. R2a has strong fundamentalists; R2b has weak fundamentalists, stronger trend followers and faster withdrawal of liquidity.

\paragraph{What a candidate simulator sees.} Six unperturbed 30-minute episodes per world: the event log (time, one of six event types, size, price level, price, and the spread and ten-level depths just before the event) and a 5-second grid of quotes and depth. It never sees the rules, the parameters, the fundamental value or the regime.

\section{Fitting the candidate simulators}
\label{app:fits}

\paragraph{Hawkes.} Baselines and the $6\times6$ branching matrix are estimated by expectation--maximisation for each decay rate on a grid from 0.02 to 2 per second, keeping the rate with the highest likelihood. The simulator's baselines are state-dependent (cancellations scale with depth, market orders with the inverse spread), and the EM uses the same factors evaluated on the logged pre-event book state. Size and price-level marks are geometric, fitted by maximum likelihood; the probability of improving the quote is the share of limit orders placed inside the spread among those submitted when the spread allowed it. On data generated by the simulator itself, the fit recovers the decay exactly and the branching matrix within 0.04. If the fitted branching ratio reaches 0.98 the matrix is rescaled to 0.95; this was not needed in any world.

\paragraph{ABIDES.} There is no likelihood. The mean price is set so that one cent equals one reference tick, and eighteen configurations (60, 102 or 180 value traders; none or two market makers; three levels of fundamental volatility) are each simulated without intervention for six seeds. The configuration with the smallest realism error against statistics of twenty further unperturbed reference runs is kept.

\paragraph{MarS.} MarS cannot be fitted. It was conditioned instead: the first thirty minutes of a MarS run replayed a reference episode's orders into MarS's exchange, after which the pretrained model generated all flow.

\section{Variance reduction in the reference worlds}
\label{app:cv}

Implementation shortfall splits exactly into the impact cost against the twin and a timing term, the twin's own drift weighted by when the policy traded. For a fixed schedule the timing term has mean zero in the reference worlds, because their rules are symmetric between buying and selling. The timing terms of TWAP, the $b=4$ front-loaded schedule and the back-loaded schedule are therefore control variates with known mean: each policy's shortfall is regressed on them across seeds and the fitted part subtracted. This leaves every policy's expected cost unchanged, including the adaptive ones, and reduces the per-seed standard deviation by between a third and two-thirds where it is large (Table~\ref{tab:reftruth}). The adjustment is applied only in the reference worlds; the candidate simulators' own estimates are plain means, as a user of those simulators would compute them.

\section{Implementation notes}

\paragraph{MarS in-process.} The model is loaded with the repository's own class and sampled with its own procedure (softmax at temperature 1, one multinomial draw); only the HTTP round trip to a Ray Serve deployment is removed. The repository's Ray import is satisfied by a stub. Child orders are marketable limit orders priced at the deepest visible level they need and never larger than the ten visible levels, since the engine has no market-order type. Rollouts are limited to 420 seconds of wall-clock time for the 50-minute prefix and 150 seconds for each 12-minute branch.

\paragraph{ABIDES.} The order-size model depends on an old release of the \texttt{pomegranate} library; a small module sampling the same mixture replaces it. To place ABIDES at a lower price, the fundamental volatility and the two shock parameters of the RMSC04 configuration are scaled with the price level; at the default price the original results are reproduced bit-for-bit.

\paragraph{Tests.} Thirteen unit tests cover the sign of costs for buys and sells, the charge for unfilled shares, clipping, lot rounding and forced completion, completion of the parent order by every candidate policy, invariance of each policy's decisions to later observations, price--time priority in the matching engine, subcriticality of the Hawkes regimes, and identity of twin and intervention before the task.
