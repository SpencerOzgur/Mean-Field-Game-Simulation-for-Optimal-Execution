1 Summary

Three questions, three answers. (A) Can simulators reproduce real market behaviour? Partly: some match observational statistics, none passes the intervention tests. (B) Can they rank execution strategies? Often not, for two reasons: the decisions are flat or under-powered (even the true generator fails at our sample size) and, in the agent-mix markets, the mechanism is missing. (C) Can observable validation tell which simulator to trust? No: a substantive negative result. Reframed question: under what conditions does predictive error turn into poor decisions, and can validation detect those conditions before optimisation?

Main thesis. Observational market realism does not identify simulators that give reliable execution advice. The only property associated with better advice is how well a simulator reproduces the market's response to the specific trade, and that association is weak: it did not survive a pre-registered confirmatory test. Optimising over a simulator whose mechanism differs from the market's makes decisions worse than plain TWAP.

  1. Realistic simulators disagree. On three Nasdaq stocks, simulators with similar realism scores recommend opposite strategies: the original Hawkes model says "sell at once", the original ABIDES model says "spread the order out" (gap 4.6 to 7.6 bps, replicated under pre-registration on CSCO and MSFT).
  2. The disagreement has a measurable cause. The decision weighs two costs: selling at once (book depth) and selling gradually (impact of the trader's own order). No simulator gets both right, and which simulator gets impact right depends on the real-data benchmark used.
  3. Realism does not predict decision quality. In 26 synthetic markets where the true best strategy is known, no realism score from LOB-Bench or from LOB-Impact's free-run phase predicts low regret. This held in a confirmatory test on ten randomly drawn markets.
  4. Impact accuracy helps a little. Accuracy on the trader's own impact correlates +0.24 [0.01, 0.30] with good advice over the first ten markets, but the primary confirmatory test on ten new markets was not met (+0.15 [−0.03, 0.28]).
  5. Optimisation amplifies misspecification. Picking the cheapest predicted strategy selects the most flattering errors (over-promise of 10.4 bps against 1.6 bps for a typical strategy). Where the market lies outside the simulators' family, this is significantly worse than TWAP (+3.75 bps); inside it, it tends to beat TWAP (−1.96 bps, not significant). Conservative rules remove the damage but, tuned on misspecified markets, also never leave TWAP.

3 Data

  • Source. Public Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH 5.0 sample days, rebuilt into LOBSTER format (messages and a ten-level book after every message), keeping only events that change the visible ten levels plus all executions.
  • Days. Fit days 2019-01-30, 2019-03-27, 2019-07-30, 2019-08-30, 2019-10-18; held-out days 2019-10-30, 2019-12-30, 2020-01-30. Simulators learn only from fit days; realism is scored on held-out days.
  • Windows. Ten-minute windows from 09:45 to 15:45 (36 per day), labelled high or low on volatility, volume and trend by median splits. A slice is usable if it has at least four fit and four held-out windows: INTC 7 slices, CSCO 5, MSFT 6.
  • Discovery and confirmation. All exploration was done on INTC; CSCO (primary) and MSFT (secondary) were run once under the pre-registration.

6 Measures

Realism flavour (observational)

  • LOB-Bench (ICML 2025, @276baf3): 21 default statistics compared by L1 (order book); conditional statistics; five return and volatility statistics by Wasserstein (price); LOB-Bench's impact score.
  • Trade response: mean price change ten seconds after the market's own aggressive orders.
  • LOB-Impact phase 1 (ICAIF 2026): trade-sign memory γ, Hurst exponent H, response ratio R(100)/R(1), free drift, from free runs.

Counterfactual flavour (interventional)

  • LOB-Impact phases 2–3: a standard 100-child metaorder (median child size, 10% participation, buy and sell pooled); build-up exponent δ (signed binned estimator, day-clustered bootstrap), amplitude Y, peak impact, relaxation (end over peak after 100 cooling windows), visible over blind attribution (Hawkes only).
  • Metaorder impact error (ours, task-specific): the simulator's TWAP peak impact against the market's own, at the task's order size.
  • Validation profile (addendum 5): immediate-execution cost, TWAP impact, relaxation after TWAP, conditional price response, and, in synthetic markets only, cost-ranking accuracy.

Real-data checks (Goal 1)

  • Cost of selling at once: walk the real held-out book (an upper bound; hidden liquidity lowers it by 7–17%).
  • TWAP impact: against the square-root law band (literature constants) and against impact measured on our own trades with synthetic metaorders.

The clock adaptation is not the cause. ABIDES-calibrated's deep book absorbs 100-share children (direct jump 0.00 bps); with 5× children impact appears. TRADES case study

In the one window TRADES covers, its realism score was close to ABIDES-original's (0.47 against 0.44) and the two disagreed by mean cost; the TRADES result was driven by occasional collapses of one side of its book, and by median it preferred front-loading. Ten sampling steps instead of one scored worse on LOB-Bench (0.555 against 0.483), and a second training seed failed to learn. TRADES is reported as a case study only.

9 Goal 2 design: markets with known answers

Each reference market is a simulator whose rules we wrote, so every strategy's true expected cost can be computed (300 seeds per strategy and size). Candidate simulators see only six 30-minute observable episodes (event log and quotes), are fitted, scored for realism against twelve held-out reference windows, and asked for a recommendation (30 seeds). Regret is the true cost of the recommendation minus the true best.

MarketParameters

The 29 candidate simulators per market

  • Hawkes family (28): every combination of spread closing, gap filling and multi-timescale kernels (8 structures), each with market-order excitation scaled 0.5, 1 or 2 (24), plus stability caps 0.8 and 0.95 for the plain and the full structure (4).

Inference

The 29 designs recur in every market, there are few markets, and each recommendation rests on 30 seeds. All Goal 2 intervals come from a three-level bootstrap (2,000 resamples): markets with replacement; designs with replacement, shared across markets; seeds within each market and design, from which predicted costs, recommendations, regret and the metaorder measure are recomputed. Realism and LOB-Impact scores are held fixed. Exact ties in predicted costs go to the first strategy by name in every code path.

Why TWAP wins: predicted against true gains

  • Shared and idiosyncratic error: 29% of the error variance is shared by the simulators; 71% is specific to a simulator.
  • Ranking before selection is poor: median rank correlation of predicted and true costs over the 12 strategies is 0.22 (queue-reactive 0.42, agent-mix 0.02).
  • Selection amplifies error (the optimizer's curse): the chosen strategy's over-promise is +10.4 bps against +1.6 bps for a typical strategy. Promised and delivered gains of non-TWAP picks correlate −0.34; only 23% of them beat TWAP.
  • Oracle: removing the shared bias (using the truth) would give 1.62 bps against TWAP's 1.89.

13 Limitations

  • Three large US stocks, eight public sample days, sell orders, market orders only, ten-minute horizon.
  • Goal 2 decisions rest on 30 seeds per candidate, which the oracle shows is too few: sampling noise alone produces regret of the order of TWAP's in agent-mix and Hawkes-built markets. Queue-reactive decisions are flat.
  • Real data cannot say which simulator is right. Ground truth comes only from synthetic markets whose rules we wrote; the two synthetic families share mechanisms (mean reversion), which a prior can exploit.
  • The well-specified comparison rests on six markets and its favourable result for simulators is not significant.
  • LOB-Impact used 64 rollouts per arm over 8 days (paper: 1,024 over 20); ABIDES ran on a time clock, slow ABIDES runs were fitted into the trading day, and the Hawkes blind arm is only partly blind.
  • The real cost of selling at once is a band, not a point (hidden liquidity, other venues); real impact depends on the benchmark used.
  • Hawkes models miss sub-millisecond bursts; ABIDES-calibrated uses data-fitted quoting agents.
  • TRADES rests on one successfully trained seed and five training days.

14 Deviations and corrections

Deviations from plans

  • Base versions ran 60 seeds instead of 20 (runner default); tests use the pre-registered 20.
  • MSFT ABIDES-calibrated at 30% finished 92 of 120 seed-sets (30% is exploratory).
  • ABIDES LOB-Impact rollouts fitted into 5.5 hours, making ABIDES-original's children up to 2.5 times denser (addendum 1).
  • LOB-Impact estimators ported, then validated against the tool (addendum 2).
  • A deep-book market (R1e) has zero five-second volatility, so LOB-Impact's amplitude is undefined there and reported as "no law".
